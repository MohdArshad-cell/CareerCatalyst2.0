# How to Beat the Workday ATS in 2026

If you've ever uploaded your perfectly designed, two-column Canva resume into Workday and watched it scramble your experience into unreadable gibberish, you are not alone. 

Workday is the most ubiquitous Applicant Tracking System (ATS) used by top tech companies, but it is notoriously bad at parsing complex layouts.

## The Problem with Modern Resumes

Most candidates think they need a flashy resume to stand out. The truth? **Recruiters never see your flashy resume if the ATS rejects it first.**

When you upload a PDF to Workday, it uses Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and text extraction algorithms to map your document into its internal database. If it encounters:
- Tables
- Multiple columns
- Icons or graphics
- Weird fonts

...it fails to map your data correctly. The recruiter sees a blank profile, and you get the automated rejection email 24 hours later.

## The Solution: Single-Column LaTeX

The only way to guarantee a 100% parse rate across Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever is to use a **single-column, text-based format**. We highly recommend using LaTeX for this.

### Why LaTeX?
1. **Clean Code Structure**: PDFs generated from LaTeX have pristine, perfectly ordered underlying text streams.
2. **Standardization**: Recruiters are used to seeing the classic "Computer Modern" font and structured layout.
3. **No Fluff**: It forces you to focus on the content—your bullets and metrics—rather than the design.

## The XYZ Framework

Once your layout is fixed, you need to fix your content. Workday scores resumes based on keyword matching. If the job description asks for "Node.js" and "Scalability", and your resume just says "Built a backend server", you will rank low.

Use the Google **XYZ Framework** for every bullet point:
> "Accomplished **[X]** as measured by **[Y]**, by doing **[Z]**."

### Bad Bullet Point
> ❌ Fixed bugs in the database to make the app faster.

### Good Bullet Point
> ✅ Engineered a high-throughput **[Z]** PostgreSQL indexing system, reducing data latency by 45% **[Y]** and serving 10,000+ concurrent users **[X]**.
